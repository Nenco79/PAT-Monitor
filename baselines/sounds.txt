# the recognition thresholds, measured on public datasets — 6 September 2026
#
# **Thresholds are not tuned on the developer's house.** A few recordings taken
# here would give thresholds right for this room, this dog and this microphone,
# and wrong for anybody else — with no way of noticing, because they would work
# perfectly well where they were chosen. What is needed is recordings labelled by
# other people, many of them, and of different provenance.
#
# What follows is CED-tiny run over three public datasets. The model is the same
# one that runs in the monitor, `internal/ced/ced-tiny-q8_0.gguf`, and the path is
# the real one: reading, resampling with `internal/resample`, front-end, forward
# pass. Parity with the oracle is in `baselines/ced.txt`.

THE THREE DATASETS, AND WHY THESE

  ESC-50                                        CC BY-NC · 600 MB · GitHub
    2000 clips, 50 classes of 40, five seconds, mono 16-bit 44.1 kHz.
    Positives: `crying_baby` 40, `dog` 40. Negatives: the other 48 classes.
    It is the benchmark almost every model of this family reports, and it is the
    only downloadable dataset that has **infant crying** as a class of its own.
      github.com/karolpiczak/ESC-50 (archive/refs/heads/master.zip)

  UrbanSound8K                                  CC BY-NC · 6.0 GB · Zenodo
    8732 clips (8708 evaluated), ten urban classes, up to four seconds, rates
    and depths **not uniform** — 8, 16, 24 and 32 bits, mono and stereo, from 8
    to 192 kHz.
    Positives: `dog_bark` 1000. The negative that matters: `children_playing`
    1000. Every excerpt carries a `salience`: 1 in the foreground, 2 in the
    background.
      zenodo.org/records/1203745

  FSD50K, the eval only                  CC-BY (per clip) · 6.2 GB · Zenodo
    10,231 clips, 200 classes of the AudioSet ontology, from 0.3 to 30 seconds.
    Positives: `Bark` 122, `Crying_and_sobbing` 42.
    It is the most **varied** of the three: 132 cats, 78 miaows, 785 speech, 253
    laughter, that is, exactly the confusions the other two suggest.
      zenodo.org/records/4060432 (ground_truth + eval_audio .z01 and .zip)

  AudioSet, which CED is trained on, **is not usable**: the audio is not
  distributed, only YouTube identifiers and some features. Having it would mean
  collecting twenty thousand videos, which is fragile and is not ours to do. The
  three datasets above are the benchmarks the models of this family report, and
  they all come from Freesound: compared with AudioSet they are different
  material, which is the property that is needed.

  What is **missing**, and it is the weak half: infant crying. In all the freely
  downloadable material there are **forty clips**, ESC-50's. FSD50K has
  `Crying_and_sobbing`, which includes adult crying, and in its 200-class
  vocabulary "Baby cry" does not exist. The specialised corpora — donateacry,
  Baby Chillanto, ICSD — classify **why** a child is crying, not whether it is,
  and none of the three has a licence permitting redistribution of what comes out
  of them.

THE CLASSES: THE WIDE LIST WAS HARMFUL

  The first list looked at everything that sounded related and took the maximum.
  **Every extra class brings its own false positives**, and two were disastrous.
  Measured on ESC-50, per single class:

    class                    positives:median   negatives:max   what causes it
    Bark                          0.508            0.019       pig
    Dog                           0.776            0.442       brushing_teeth
    Domestic animals, pets        0.685            0.877       cat
    Bow-wow                       0.368            0.117       pig
    Baby cry, infant cry          0.574            0.136       cat
    Crying, sobbing               0.391            0.181       cat
    Screaming                     0.002            0.204       coughing

  "Domestic animals, pets" gives 0.877 on a cat: including it means importing its
  false positives. "Dog" is the strongest on the positives and catches
  `brushing_teeth` at 0.442. "Screaming" is worth two thousandths on the
  positives.

  What is left:
    bark = max(Bark, Bow-wow)
    cry  = max(Baby cry infant cry, Crying sobbing)

  On ESC-50, at threshold 0.10, "Bark + Dog" makes **15 false positives** where
  "Bark + Bow-wow" makes one. The difference is not the threshold: it is the
  list.

  2026, measured in the last section of this file.

  **The bark holds up on three independent datasets, the cry on forty clips.**
  The two FSD50K rows for the cry are weak and are to be read for what they are:
  42 clips of `Crying_and_sobbing`, which is adult crying as much as infant, and
  the model is trained to tell them apart. It is not a refutation of the number,
  it is the absence of a confirmation.

  The low recall on UrbanSound8K is not the model's, and it is measured: the
  `salience` explains it on its own — 82% on foreground barks, 36% on background
  ones, and under a second of duration 29%. A dog three blocks away covered by
  traffic is a clip legitimately labelled `dog_bark` and is not what this program
  has to catch: that one is a dog **in the room**.

  The same holds for FSD50K, where the recall depends on the clip's duration:
  inside the window 81%, above the window 46% — because there the model splits
  and averages the logits at equal weight, and it is the dilution below seen from
  the other side. **Before believing a low recall one looks at what it was fed.**

THE DILUTION, WHICH IS THE RESULT THAT CHANGES THE DESIGN

  The datasets' clips last five seconds and the event fills them. The monitor's
  window lasts 10.11 and a real event occupies a fraction of it. Measured on
  twenty positives per kind, the event placed at the centre of a whole window,
  first in silence and then on a room's floor at -46 dBFS:

    event     cry (median)            bark (median)
              silence / room          silence / room
     5.0 s     0.819  /  0.378         0.497  /  0.563
     2.0 s     0.657  /  0.173         0.476  /  0.561
     1.0 s     0.299  /  0.025         0.421  /  0.529
     0.5 s     0.050  /  0.006         0.385  /  0.461

  **The bark does not dilute and the cry collapses.** Half a second of barking
  inside ten is still worth 0.46; one second of crying is worth 0.025, that is,
  it is invisible. A bark is a burst with a signature of its own, a cry is a long
  shape the model recognises only if there is enough of it.

  Two design consequences follow that no five-second clip could have revealed:

  1. **The model is not asked at the instant the gate opens.** There the window
     holds one second of event and nine of what came before. It is asked
     afterwards, when the event has filled part of the window.

  2. **For the cry the lever is not the threshold, it is time.** Raising it to
     0.30 would cover ESC-50's cat (0.181) and UrbanSound8K's children playing
     (0.292), but the positives diluted in a real room sit at the tenth
     percentile at 0.166: precisely the case that matters would be lost. The
     answer is to require the threshold to be exceeded over **two consecutive
     windows** — a cat miaows once, a child cries for minutes. It is the same
     thing that already separates cry and bark in `internal/detect`: "the only
     thing that separates them is time".
     It does not hold for the bark: a bark is a short episode, and demanding two
     windows would mean losing it.

THE GATE: WHAT IT LETS THROUGH, AND WHICH KNOB MATTERS

  `internal/detect`'s shape detector decides when to wake the model, and **it is
  judged the other way round**: what matters about the model is that it does not
  get things wrong, about the gate that it loses nothing — what does not pass
  here the model will never see. The price of letting too much through is not a
  false alarm, it is half a second of CPU per passage.

  Measured with `pat-sounds gate`, which brings every clip **to the same level
  above a floor settled at -46 dBFS**. It is a choice and it has to be
  understood: the absolute level of a dataset clip means nothing — it depends on
  how whoever uploaded it to Freesound had their gain set — while the gate's
  threshold is a distance from the **room's** floor. Bringing them all to the
  same level, the threshold in dB stops being the variable, and the two things a
  dataset can really teach are left: **the band and the shape in time**.

  With band 150-2500 Hz, 12 dB above the floor, and two hits — which was the
  tuning when these rows were taken:

    ESC-50, positives          cry 97% pass, bark 82% pass
    ESC-50, what passes anyway   rooster 100%, laughing 87%, hen 82%,
                                 footsteps 82%, keyboard_typing 80%
    ESC-50, what it stops       rain 0%, wind 5%, crickets 5%,
                                 washing_machine 7%, helicopter 7%,
                                 airplane 12%, train 12%, vacuum_cleaner 15%
    UrbanSound8K, what it stops engine_idling 0%, air_conditioner 2%,
                                 jackhammer 3%, gun_shot 2%, car_horn 5%,
                                 siren 8%

  **What the gate really separates is the intermittent from the continuous**, and
  it does that well: an engine, an air conditioner, rain almost never get
  through. What it does not separate is a bark from a cockerel, from laughter or
  from a keyboard — and that is not a defect, it is the model's job.

  THE BAND IS ALMOST INERT
    150-2500 Hz    negatives that pass 47.7%   cry 92.5%  bark 65%
    0-8000 Hz      negatives that pass 51.6%   cry 92.5%  bark 65%
    120-1640 Hz    negatives that pass 42.0%   cry 80.0%  bark 65%

    Removing the band altogether costs **four points** more negatives and does
    not touch the positives: that criterion buys far less than it seems, because
    almost every sound has a zero-crossing rate inside that range. Narrowing it
    to the 120-1640 Hz of the physiological literature costs **12.5 points of
    recall on the cry** to gain 5.7 of rejection: that is the wrong direction for
    a gate. **It stays as it is** because it is almost free, not because it does
    much.

  THE HITS ARE THE REAL LEVER, AND THEY COST RECALL
    ESC-50, threshold 12 dB:
      one hit     cry 100.0%  bark 85.0%  pass 59.0%
      two hits    cry  92.5%  bark 65.0%  pass 47.7%
      three hits  cry  80.0%  bark 50.0%  pass 38.4%
    UrbanSound8K, threshold 12 dB (1000 barks instead of 40):
      one hit     bark 54.4%  pass 15.2%
      two hits    bark 35.2%  pass  9.1%
      three hits  bark 18.8%  pass  8.0%

    With **one hit alone the recall on the cry is 100% at any threshold**, and
    the bark gains twenty points on ESC-50 and nineteen on UrbanSound8K.

  HENCE THE CONCLUSION, AND IT HAS BEEN APPLIED
    With the model downstream the answer is **one hit**: there the recall is
    everything, because the model is what decides and an event the gate loses is
    lost for ever.

    That conclusion could not be applied while the gate's own verdict became the
    alert: at one hit it would have sounded on 59% of a room's loud sounds. **The
    two knobs moved together with the model**, and today they are both moved:
    `internal/ced` decides, and `DefaultBarkHits` and `DefaultCryHits` are 1.

  THE COMPARISON WITH URBANSOUND8K HAS A LIMIT, AND IT HAS TO BE SAID
    There the recall and the negatives that pass **fall together** — 85% and 59%
    on ESC-50 against 54% and 15% — and two columns that fall together are not
    the signature of wrong thresholds. UrbanSound8K's clips are excerpts of
    street recordings: normalising the whole clip also raises its floor above the
    threshold, and the gate stops seeing a burst because it sees an unbroken
    sound. ESC-50, which is isolated events on a silent floor, is the right
    material for this question — and it is also a bedroom's condition.

  WHAT THE DATASET CANNOT TEACH
    The distance from the floor. It is a property of the **room**, not of the
    sound, and the datasets do not have one: the useful number is the one
    measured here with the real microphone, a floor at -46 dBFS with the room
    empty, which is in `baselines/ced.txt`. The twelve dB stay, and what moves
    them will at most be a measurement in a different room.

  HOW OFTEN IT OPENS, IN A REAL ROOM
    Three minutes from this machine's microphone, at night, room with nobody in
    it:

                                          two hits       one hit
      100 ms blocks                         1796           1798
      settled floor                       -44.9 dBFS     -40.9 dBFS
      blocks above the threshold            11 (0.61%)     31 (1.72%)
      times the gate opened                 **zero**       **six**

    **The left-hand column was taken with the earlier tuning, and no longer
    holds.** With two hits the gate never opened and the model's cost at rest was
    zero; with one — which is today's tuning — a single isolated hit is enough,
    and six arrived in three minutes. The honest number is **1.7% of a core**:
    six half-second classifications in a hundred and eighty-one seconds.
    (The floor was also four dB higher, so the two columns do not differ by the
    tuning alone — but the direction is not in doubt, because one hit opens where
    two did not.)

    **The count is per event and not per hour**, and that does not change: half a
    second every time the gate opens, with the cap of one classification every
    five seconds. The worst case is unbroken crying, where that cap is worth 10%
    of a core for as long as it lasts.

    **A review found the correction, not a remeasurement**: the number zero had
    been taken two commits before the tuning changed, and nobody had redone it. A
    measurement is attributed to the configuration it was taken in, otherwise it
    outlives the thing it described.

    A room with a street under the window would give another number, and that is
    why this line says **where** it was taken.

HOW IT IS REDONE

  The datasets are not in the repository and must not be. They are downloaded
  from the three addresses above and passed through `pat-sounds`, which writes one
  CSV per dataset — one row per clip, one column per candidate class — and then
  judges it. The pass costs: 2m43s for ESC-50, 9m03s for UrbanSound8K, 37m46s for
  FSD50K, with four workers on this machine.

  **Everything is written down and decided afterwards**: the pass is expensive and
  the questions are many — which subset of classes, which threshold, which
  aggregation — and at no cost one can change one's mind.

  The number to look at first is **how many clips were evaluated**, not the
  scores: 2000 out of 2000 for ESC-50, 8708 out of 8732 for UrbanSound8K, 10,231
  for FSD50K. A WAV reader that refuses a format skips those clips in silence,
  and the recall that follows looks like the model's — it happened: 45% of
  UrbanSound8K declares `WAVE_FORMAT_EXTENSIBLE`, which is not a format but an
  indirection to the real code inside the subformat GUID.

LIVE, THE WHOLE CHAIN — 6 September 2026

  Real ESC-50 clips played through the speakers and picked up by this machine's
  microphone, with the monitor running: speaker, room, microphone, analysis at
  16 kHz, gate, model, alert. **It is the only test that crosses the air too.**

  **The conditions were adverse, and that has to be said because it changes how
  the numbers are read**: a noisy room and a low playback volume. What follows is
  therefore a **floor** and not a ceiling — the sound reached the microphone
  attenuated and on a floor higher than a night-time one, and the model
  recognised it all the same. A real bark in the room, at its own distance and
  volume, sits above these values.

  eight `dog` clips
    0.000 → 0.143 → 0.266 → 0.343 → 0.435 (class Bow-wow, then Bark)
    `bark` alert at the first verdict above 0.20
    cry **0.000 throughout**

  eight `crying_baby` clips
    0.020 → 0.323 (first confirmation, no alert) → 0.430 (second) → `cry` alert;
    then 0.590, 0.417, 0.422
    bark **0.000 throughout**

  Three things that can be seen only here. **The dilution at work**: the score
  rises as the window fills with the sound, it does not jump. **The two-
  confirmation rule**: it costs one window, that is about five seconds, and it is
  visible in the row at 0.323 that raised nothing. **And the two events do not
  cross**: none of the sixteen classifications gave more than zero to the wrong
  event.

  Cost per window, measured with everything else running: 502-550 ms.

THE FOURTH SET, AND WHAT IT DID TO THE WEAK HALF — 11 September 2026

  The paragraph above says the freely downloadable material holds **forty**
  infant cries, ESC-50's, and that the specialised corpora classify **why** a
  child is crying rather than whether it is. The first is wrong and the second
  is true and beside the point: **the labels are useless to us and the audio is
  not.** What was needed was positives, and a corpus labelled by cause is a
  corpus of positives with the labels thrown away.

  donateacry-corpus                            ODbL · 50 MB · GitHub
    457 clips in the curated subset, 8 kHz mono 16-bit, about seven seconds
    each, 53 minutes in all: 382 hungry, 27 discomfort, 24 tired, 16 belly
    pain, 8 burping. Collected through a mobile app and published, in the
    authors' words, "in their original, unmodified, unchecked form".
    Positives only: it brings no negatives, which is the half already covered
    ten thousand clips over.
      github.com/gveres/donateacry-corpus

  **The licence objection does not hold either.** The note above says none of
  the three corpora permits redistributing what comes out of them; the ODbL
  permits producing works from the database with attribution, and what leaves
  this repository is a table of numbers — no audio of anybody's is
  redistributed, and none is in the repository. It is a lighter condition than
  the CC BY-NC of the two sets already in use.

  **It is not AudioSet**, which is the property that matters: CED is trained on
  AudioSet, so a corpus drawn from it would measure memory rather than skill.
  That is why ICSD was left out although it is the best fit on paper — 338 real
  ten-second clips, strongly labelled, which is exactly this window — its cries
  come from Baby Chillanto, donateacry **and AudioSet** with no per-clip
  attribution, so the contamination cannot be separated out. CryCeleb2023 was
  left out for a different reason: 26k files and 6.5 hours, but they are
  manually segmented **expirations** from newborns in hospital within a day of
  birth, that is, the wrong granularity and a very narrow condition.

  WHAT IT SAYS, WHOLE CLIPS

    positives n=457   min 0.000   p10 0.069   median 0.561

    threshold      recall
        0.10     393/457 (85%)
        0.15     383/457 (83%)
        0.20     363/457 (79%)
        0.30     329/457 (71%)

    against ESC-50's forty, which give 39/40 (97%) at 0.20.

    By cause, which is the one use the labels have: belly pain is the hardest
    — 16 clips, median 0.326, 56% at 0.20 — and discomfort the easiest, 92%.
    The bulk is hunger, 382 clips at 79%.

  AND TWO OF THE 457 ANNOUNCE A DOG

    On a corpus that is nothing but infant crying, two clips put `bark` above
    0.20 — 0.303 and 0.206 — with `cry` at 0.005 and 0.001. That is, the cry
    does not fire and the dog does: a pure false alarm, and **the bark asks for
    one window where the cry asks for two**, so nothing downstream catches it.

    0.44% of the recordings, and the reading is not settled. It may be the
    model, and it may be the corpus: these samples are published, in the
    authors' own words, "in their original, unmodified, unchecked form", and a
    clip the model scores at 0.005 for crying may simply not contain a cry. The
    two do not separate without listening to them, which is why this is written
    as a measurement and not as a defect.

  TWO EXPLANATIONS WERE MEASURED AND BOTH FELL

    A lower recall on another corpus has three candidate causes and they
    predict the same number, so two were removed by experiment rather than by
    argument.

    **Not the band.** These recordings are 8 kHz, so everything above 4 kHz is
    missing from them. The same forty ESC-50 clips with their spectrum zeroed
    above 4 kHz — one variable moved, the rate and the length left alone —
    give **40/40 at 0.20**, min 0.218, median 0.657 against the 0.599 they give
    whole. Removing the top half of the spectrum does not cost anything here;
    it gains a little.

    **Not the level.** The medians are 1.8 dB apart (ESC-50 -20.0 dBFS,
    donateacry -21.8), but the **tails** are not: the tenth percentile is -22.6
    against **-36.6**. Bringing every event to -20 dBFS before mixing — what
    `gate` already does, and now `dilute -level` — moves donateacry's diluted
    median from 0.204 to 0.220 and ESC-50's from 0.323 to 0.324. That is, it
    changes nothing, and the fact that it changes nothing on the set that was
    already at that level is what says the normalisation works.

    What is left is the material: **cries recorded by parents on a phone are
    harder than forty curated clips**, and the hard ones are quieter and
    sparser — the lost ones sit at -28.3 dBFS filling 39% of their clip, the
    caught ones at -20.9 dBFS filling 52%.

  THE DILUTION, RE-MEASURED — AND THE PUBLISHED NUMBER WAS TWO CLIPS

    Five seconds of cry at the centre of a whole window, on a room floor of
    -46 dBFS:

      positives                                   median      p10
      ESC-50, 20 — the number published above       0.378     0.166
      ESC-50, 40 — all it has                       0.323     0.082
      ESC-50, 40, every event at -20 dBFS           0.324     0.079
      donateacry, 150                               0.204     0.004
      donateacry, 150, every event at -20 dBFS      0.220     0.005

    **Doubling the sample halved the tenth percentile**, and that on its own
    settles what kind of number 0.166 was: the tenth percentile of twenty
    clips is the second value in the list, which is one sample wearing a
    statistic's name. On a hundred and fifty it is the fifteenth.

    What it does to the design is the opposite of weakening it. The rule that
    the threshold must be exceeded on **two consecutive windows** was argued
    from 0.166; at a diluted median of 0.204 the argument is much stronger,
    because half of the windows of a real cry fall on the wrong side of 0.20
    on their own. It still works for the reason it was written: a cry lasts
    minutes and the recogniser is asked every five seconds, so a window that
    fails is followed by others. What could not survive is raising the
    threshold to 0.30, which the diluted median is now below.

    **And the question this opens is not answered here.** Lowering it — 0.15
    gives 83% instead of 79% on whole clips — cannot be decided from
    positives: it needs the false ones re-measured over UrbanSound8K and
    FSD50K, which is twelve gigabytes and another day. It is written among the
    open questions rather than guessed at.

  WHAT IS STILL MISSING

    One more provenance. The bark stands on three independent sets; the cry now
    stands on 497 clips from **two**, and donateacry is one collection method —
    a phone, close, in a home. It is no longer forty, and it is not three.

  HOW IT WAS DONE

    pat-sounds score -kind donateacry -dir ...\donateacry -out donateacry.csv
    pat-sounds judge -kind donateacry -scores donateacry.csv
    pat-sounds dilute -kind donateacry -dir ...\donateacry -bg ...\ESC-50-master -n 150
    pat-sounds dilute -kind donateacry -dir ... -bg ... -n 150 -level -20

    `-bg` is there because the room background is ESC-50's `clock_tick` and a
    corpus of cries has nothing of the kind: without it the tool refuses, which
    is right — the column "on the room background" would otherwise have
    measured the event in silence and looked like the answer to a different
    question.

    **And `dilute` had a resampler built for 44.1 kHz applied to whatever
    arrived.** With ESC-50 it was correct because every clip there is 44.1; on
    an 8 kHz corpus it would have stretched every event by five and a half and
    scored something else entirely — a measurement that answers, and answers
    about another sound. One resampler per rate now, as `score` already did.

THE ROOM ITSELF, WHICH NO OTHER SET CARRIES — 11 September 2026

  Every corpus here was recorded close to the sound. The monitor is not: it is a
  laptop on the other side of a bedroom, off to one side, in a room with walls.
  That axis had never been measured, and one dataset carries it.

  CSIBE-AIBO                                   CC BY 4.0 · 355 MB · Zenodo
    CSIBE-RAW played back through a good speaker and **recorded again** by the
    microphones of a Sony ERS-7 robot, in four configurations: speaker at 1 m
    and 30 degrees off the head, and at 3 m, 1 m high, 180 degrees — that is,
    behind it — each in a reverberant and a non-reverberant room. `baby cry` is
    one of its fourteen classes.
      zenodo.org/records/1243714 (csibe_aibo_20180508.zip)

  **The sample is eight recordings, not 240 clips.** Each configuration holds
  240 files and they are pieces of eight cries, one of which is 125 of them.
  Counting the files would have given a sample five times larger than the one
  there is; it is stated here because the first version of this measurement
  made that mistake.

  **And the four sessions are not at one level**: they were recorded on four
  days with different gains, 1 m dry at -29.5 dBFS and 3 m dry at -50.4. So
  every window is brought to -20 dBFS before being scored, and what is left
  between the four is the room.

  WHAT IT SAYS

    The fragments are put back in order into the cry they came from and cut
    into windows of the model's own length — 21 windows per configuration,
    which is what eight recordings give.

      configuration       median   p10     min
      1 m, dry             0.614   0.432   0.140
      3 m, dry             0.582   0.397   0.128
      1 m, reverberant     0.493   0.291   0.176
      3 m, reverberant     0.604   0.267   0.062

    **At equal level the room costs almost nothing.** All four sit above the
    threshold of 0.20 at the median and at the tenth percentile. The ordering
    does not hold together — 3 m reverberant scores above 1 m reverberant —
    and with 21 windows from 8 recordings that is what it should be read as:
    the differences between configurations are noise, and no reverberation
    effect can be claimed from this.

    **What it does say is where the distance bites.** Three metres off-axis
    costs 20.9 dB of level and almost nothing of shape, and the level is
    exactly where the other corpus loses its recall — the cries donateacry
    misses sit at -28.3 dBFS. So distance harms through the level and not
    through the acoustics, which is the half the gate already governs with its
    twelve dB above the floor.

    **Contamination does not touch that conclusion.** CSIBE's sources were
    "collected from the internet", so an overlap with what CED was trained on
    cannot be excluded and a high score is weak evidence on its own. But it
    would help all four configurations equally, and what is read here is the
    comparison between them.

  THE FIRST ATTEMPT WAS WRONG, AND THE CONTROL IS WHAT SAID SO

    The clips are about a second long, so the first version repeated each one
    until it filled the window. That gave medians of 0.087, 0.066, 0.028 and
    0.012 — a factor of seven between dry and reverberant, which reads as a
    strong result and is an artefact.

    The control: **the same treatment applied to ESC-50's forty cries**, which
    are known to score 0.599 whole. Cut to a second, tiled and levelled, they
    give **0.003** — below every CSIBE configuration. The treatment, not the
    room, was what the numbers were measuring, and comparing 0.087 with 0.012
    where a known-good positive gives 0.003 is comparing noise with noise.

    It is the dilution chapter from the other side: a cry is a long shape, and
    a second of it repeated ten times is a repetition, not a shape. **A
    manipulation that flattens everything looks exactly like a phenomenon that
    flattens everything, and only running the known case through the same
    manipulation separates them.**

  WHAT WAS LOOKED AT AND NOT USED

    Four more were examined while looking for a third provenance of crying, and
    none of them is one:

      Mendeley "Infant's Cry Sound"  CC BY 4.0, three classes — hungry, tired,
        uncomfortable — which are donateacry's own, that is, the same
        provenance under another name.
      Jaclyn-Foo/Baby-Crying-Detection-Database  extracted from YouTube, with
        no licence and no paper: the same unseparable overlap with what CED was
        trained on as ICSD, plus nothing to rely on.
      TUT Rare sound events (DCASE 2017)  `baby cry` is one of its three target
        classes and the isolated events are a single 639 MB archive rather than
        the 17 GB of mixtures. Its `FREESOUNDCREDITS.txt` is the point: the
        events come from Freesound, which is where ESC-50 and FSD50K come from.
        Another selection of the same source, not another source.
      CryCeleb2023  6.5 hours from 786 newborns, and they are manually
        segmented **expirations** recorded in hospital within a day of birth:
        the wrong granularity and a very narrow condition. CC BY-NC-ND, gated.

    So the third provenance is still missing, and what was gained instead is an
    axis nobody had measured at all.

HOW FAR ABOVE THE ROOM A SOUND HAS TO BE — 11 September 2026

  Every corpus here is recorded close to the sound, so every number above
  describes a loud event. The monitor's events are not loud: they are across a
  bedroom. What was never measured is where the chain stops answering, and this
  is that curve.

  **Turning the volume down on the clips does not measure it.** The front end
  normalises on the window's own maximum, so a file made quieter is mostly the
  same file to the model: measured, taking 20 dB off all 457 cries moves the
  median from 0.561 to 0.483, that is, 14% for a factor of ten. What has to fall
  is the **distance from the room's floor**, with the floor left where the room
  really puts it — which is what `pat-sounds dilute -sweep` does: five seconds
  of event at the centre of a whole window, the background fixed at -46 dBFS,
  and the event walked down towards it.

    above the floor        cry, 150 real      cry, ESC-50's 40      bark, 40
                          median  >=0.20     median  >=0.20     median  >=0.20
      +30 dB               0.253    58%       0.375    85%       0.553   100%
      +24 dB               0.207    53%       0.311    78%       0.524   100%
      +18 dB               0.160    38%       0.240    55%       0.478    95%
      +12 dB               0.098    25%       0.158    40%       0.407    92%
       +9 dB               0.074    15%       0.138    32%       0.360    92%
       +6 dB               0.044     6%       0.102     8%       0.313    88%
       +3 dB               0.025     1%       0.071     0%       0.272    72%
        0 dB               0.012     0%       0.042     0%       0.222    52%
       -3 dB               0.006     0%       0.017     0%       0.185    38%

  **The bark is still heard at the room's own level, and below it.** At 0 dB —
  an event no louder than the background — half of the barks are still over the
  threshold, and at -3 dB more than a third. The cry is gone by +6 dB. That is
  the dilution asymmetry again and much larger: a bark is a burst with a
  signature of its own, a cry is a long shape at low contrast, and low contrast
  is exactly what a quiet room takes away.

  **THE GATE OPENS AT +12 dB, AND AT +12 dB THE CRY IS AT 25%.**

  `internal/detect` lets a sound through at twelve decibels above the floor, and
  that number was chosen where it belongs — measuring the floor of a real room
  with the real microphone — and it does what a gate must do, which is to lose
  nothing. **What nobody had measured is what happens on the other side**: at
  the gate's own threshold the recogniser answers for a quarter of the cries and
  for 92% of the barks. The chain's real sensitivity is set by the model, not by
  the gate, and for the cry it sits somewhere between +18 and +24 dB — which is
  two octaves of loudness away from where the gate opens.

  It is not a defect of the gate, and this is not a reason to move it: a gate
  that let less through would lose what the model can still catch. It is that
  **one threshold serves two detectors that need numbers twenty decibels
  apart**, and the cry's half of that had never been written down.

  WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY

    **It does not say the monitor cannot hear a child across a room.** It is a
    sensitivity curve, and turning it into an answer needs the other half: how
    far above the floor a real cry actually sits at two or three metres. That is
    still unmeasured and still needs a room — a real cry, or a speaker driven to
    a realistic level. What can be said arithmetically is the exchange rate:
    three metres off-axis cost 20.9 dB, so a cry sitting at +30 dB from a metre
    away is at +9 dB from three, which is the row where the cry is at 15%.

    And the lower threshold the open questions ask about is worth a little more
    here than it looked: the gap between 0.20 and 0.15 is exactly where these
    rows are thickest.

THE THRESHOLD MOVES TO 0.15 — 12 September 2026

  The question that was left open: on whole clips 0.15 catches 83% of donateacry's
  real cries against 79%, and whether that is worth having could not be decided
  from the positives. The two sets that carry the negatives were downloaded and
  re-scored — 12.3 GB, 8708 clips of UrbanSound8K and 10,231 of FSD50K, 57 minutes
  of passes — and the answer is that **both codes gain, and one number serves
  both**.

  WHAT IS CAUGHT

    cry     donateacry, 457 real cries     363 (79%)  ->  383 (83%)     +20
            FSD50K, Crying_and_sobbing      13 (30%)  ->   15 (35%)      +2
            ESC-50, crying_baby             39 (97%)  ->   39 (97%)       0
    bark    UrbanSound8K, dog_bark         660 (66%)  ->  698 (69%)     +38
            FSD50K, Bark                    74 (60%)  ->   77 (63%)      +3
            ESC-50, dog                     38 (95%)  ->   38 (95%)       0

    cry     ESC-50            0/1960   ->  1/1960     the cat, 0.181
            UrbanSound8K      1/8708   ->  4/8708     three children_playing
            FSD50K            2/10,189 ->  4/10,189   a door squeak, a gasp
    bark    ESC-50            0/1960   ->  0/1960

                                                       without the Bark label)

  **Sixty-three more real events for eight more false ones**, over some twenty
  thousand negative clips per code. As a rate: the cry goes from 0.014% to
  0.043%, the bark from 0.066% to 0.076%.

  AND THE NEXT STEP DOWN IS WHERE IT TURNS

    from 0.20 to 0.15    +63 caught    +8 false
    from 0.15 to 0.10    +49 caught   +22 false

  The knee is at 0.15. It was not chosen for being a round number between the
  two: 0.10 was measured in the same pass and costs nearly three times as much
  for less.

  WHAT ENTERS, BY NAME

    A count hides what a name shows. At 0.15 the new false positives for the cry
    are ESC-50's cat at 0.181; children_playing at 0.185, 0.178 and 0.160 on
    UrbanSound8K, in a class whose worst clip was already through at 0.292; and
    on FSD50K a door squeak at 0.192 and a gasp at 0.187. **Nothing new in
    kind** — every one of them is a confusion the old threshold already had at
    its own edge, and the two worst negatives of all, a bird at 0.554 and a
    shouting man at 0.252, were through at 0.20 and are untouched by this.

  WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE

    The two-window rule is argued from "a cat miaows once", and that argument has
    never been measured — it matters more now that the threshold sits below the
    cat. The window is 10.11 s and the question is asked every 5, so consecutive
    classifications overlap by half a window and one miaow is seen twice; the two
    confirmations need not be consecutive either. What may still save it is
    dilution, which is brutal. And it does not apply at all to the three
    children_playing: children playing are not an event that happens once.

  HOW IT WAS RUN

    pat-sounds score -kind urbansound8k -dir DIR\UrbanSound8K -out us8k.csv
    pat-sounds score -kind fsd50k       -dir DIR\FSD50K       -out fsd.csv
    pat-sounds judge -kind urbansound8k -scores us8k.csv
    pat-sounds judge -kind fsd50k       -scores fsd.csv

    8708 of 8732 clips evaluated on UrbanSound8K — the same 24 skipped as the
    first time, sixteen shorter than one patch and eight at 4 bits per sample —
    and 10,231 of 10,231 on FSD50K. The passes took 9m and 47m49s with six
    workers.

THE THIRD PROVENANCE IS NOT GOING TO BE DOWNLOADED — 12 September 2026

  Four candidates were excluded above, and what stayed open was whether anything
  could simply be fetched. The roads were counted instead of guessed, and the way
  they divide is itself the answer.

  FREE, AND ALREADY HERE

    Kaggle warcoder/infant-cry-audio-corpus   describes itself, in its own words,
      as "built through the Donate-a-cry campaign". It is donateacry, mirrored —
      and most of the "baby cry" sets on Kaggle are re-uploads of that or of
      ESC-50. Taking one would add a source that is not a source.
    ICSD   open, and part of its cries come from AudioSet, which CED is trained
      on. Its Hugging Face copy is gated as well.
    BigSoundBank, archive.org, effects libraries   the family Freesound belongs
      to, which is where ESC-50 and FSD50K already come from.

  FREE, INDEPENDENT, AND THE WRONG SHAPE

    CryCeleb2023   a click-through on Hugging Face with no human approval, so it
      is the one thing here that can really be downloaded today. 786 infants,
      26k files, 6.5 hours — and the card says it ships manually segmented
      expirations only, from infants born in one country. The exclusion recorded
      above survives reading the licence page.

  INDEPENDENT, AND EVERY ONE BEHIND A GATE

    HomeBank de Barbaro Cry Corpus   a Google form, a commitment to delete the
      audio when the work is done, and a citation. 22 infants of 1-10 months, a
      LENA recorder in a vest worn by the child, up to 72 h per family at home:
      61.3 h labelled in 44,129 segments of 5 s, over 7 h of them crying, with
      an inter-rater kappa of 0.847.
    Baby Chillanto   a request to INAOE. 2268 clips, Mexican intensive care,
      built for asphyxia and deafness.
    OxVoc   a form emailed to the author, for 173 sounds across every category —
      some thirty cries, fewer than ESC-50's forty, which this file already
      records as not being a sample.
    IIIT-S ICSD   no distribution: write to the authors.
    SPLAN, iCOPE, ChatterBaby   no published route at all.
    Nexdata, Axon Data   sold. The 50 hours listed on Kaggle are a vendor's
      window onto a commercial set.

  WHAT THE DIVISION MEANS

    The gate is not an accident: it is the price of the independence. These are
    recordings of identifiable infants, made by researchers with an ethics
    committee behind them, so a corpus that can sit on a public URL is by
    construction one whose audio somebody else had already collected from a
    library — that is, one of the two provenances already here.

    The division is **three-way and not two**, and the middle row is why: free
    and independent is possible, and the one corpus that is both ships the
    wrong thing. Saying "free and independent exclude each other" would be
    tidier and would walk straight past CryCeleb2023, two paragraphs above.

    So the third provenance is closed, rather than left open as though the
    search had merely been shallow.

    What the search did turn up is a **different** question, and it was left
    open instead. Every negative in this file comes from somebody else's
    street or from Freesound, and the dilution above assembles an event at the
    centre of a window over a floor measured on another evening: it simulates
    the condition the monitor works in. de Barbaro is a recording of it, and
    what is worth the form is not its seven hours of crying but the other 54,
    home audio that is **not** crying from the same rooms and continuous. Its
    two caveats are known in advance: it merges *fussing* into *crying*, so a
    recall read off those labels understates us, and the microphone rides on the
    child's chest, so it says nothing about three metres.
